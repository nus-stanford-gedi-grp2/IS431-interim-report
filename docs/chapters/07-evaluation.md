## 6. Evaluation {#evaluation}

### Feedback and validation {#feedback-validation}

To evaluate the playbook's practical relevance and usability, we sought feedback from industry professionals at The Ridge NUS and Ascott. Their feedback provided insights into the playbook's potential applications, strengths, and areas for improvement.

#### Feedback from The Ridge NUS

We sought feedback from **Mr Sherwin Poh**, who responded positively to the playbook, highlighting its potential to guide hotel operators towards more comprehensive and informed decisions during kiosk implementation.

> "It's (the playbook's) great, I would have used it as a guide to make more informed considerations when planning as there may have been things I didn't think about."
>
> ~ Mr Sherwin Poh, Senior Manager (Operations), The Ridge NUS

Mr Poh highlighted several features that could support hotel operators throughout the planning process.

1. The **Kiosk Suitability Assessment** provides a structured evaluation of whether self-service kiosks are appropriate for a hotel. He noted that the resulting suitability rating could help operators justify their recommendations and secure buy-in from senior management.

2. The **Kiosk Placement Tool** allows operators to visualise guest flow and compare different kiosk locations. This could facilitate more informed placement decisions by helping operators assess alternative lobby configurations.

3. The **Cost-Benefit Calculator** provides an initial financial assessment of kiosk implementation. Mr Poh noted that this could be particularly useful in supporting applications for Singapore Tourism Board (STB) grants.

#### Feedback from Ascott

We also sought feedback from **Mr Michael Lee**, who described the playbook as a good effort and expressed his intention to share it with Ascott's building managers.
He made the following comments:

1. The **Design Principles** section is a particularly useful component of the playbook, recognising its value in guiding kiosk design decisions.

2. The **Cost-Benefit Analysis** is helpful for managers. Its value could be further enhanced through integration with Ascott's existing databases on Power BI. Such integration could allow financial evaluations to draw directly from operational data, reducing the need for manual inputs.

3. One area for improvement concerned the **User Persona Development** section. Mr Lee noted that it was not immediately clear that the personas were intended to represent each hotel's most common guest archetypes, identified using its own guest data. This highlighted the need for clearer instructions to help operators understand how to develop representative personas.

#### Overall Takeaways

The feedback from both industry professionals was encouraging, highlighting the playbook's potential to support hotel operators in making more informed kiosk implementation decisions. In particular, the design guidance, placement visualisation, and financial evaluation tools were identified as valuable components.

The feedback also revealed opportunities to improve the playbook's clarity and integration with existing hotel systems, providing useful directions for future refinement.

### Areas for Improvement {#areas-for-improvement}

While the playbook provides a structured approach to kiosk implementation, several limitations remain. These primarily stem from limited access to hotel-specific operational data and the need for further validation by industry professionals.

#### 1. Limited Data for Kiosk Suitability Assessment

The **Kiosk Suitability Assessment** currently assigns equal weights to the guest attributes used in evaluating kiosk suitability. However, different attributes may have varying levels of influence on kiosk adoption, and their relative importance may differ across hotels.

Due to limited access to hotel-specific guest data, these weights have not been empirically validated. Further data collection and analysis would be required to refine the weighting system and improve the accuracy of suitability assessments.

#### 2. Subjectivity in Kiosk Placement Evaluation

The **Kiosk Placement Tool** currently evaluates potential kiosk locations using a set of criteria and scoring logic developed by the team. While these are based on reasonable design considerations, the ratings remain partly subjective and may not fully capture the complexities of actual hotel environments.

Further consultation with **interior designers and experienced hotel managers** would help validate the evaluation criteria, identify overlooked considerations, and improve the tool's applicability across different lobby layouts and operational contexts.

#### 3. Scope of the Execution Framework

The **Execution section** was developed based on initial fieldwork, conversations with hotel staff, and secondary research. While the four identified workstreams cover key aspects of kiosk implementation, they may not be exhaustive. Certain critical activities or finer operational details may have been overlooked.

Further input from **hotel managers with experience in technology adoption and implementation** would be valuable in validating the framework's completeness and refining the guidance within each workstream.
