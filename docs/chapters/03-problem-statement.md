## 3. Problem Statement {#problem-statement}

### Refining the problem space {#problem-space}

We were given a deliberately broad challenge:

> **How can we reimagine the hotel arrival experience with AI and Emerging Technologies?**

Taken at face value, this question could lead almost anywhere: AI concierges, robotic porters, biometric room access, predictive pre-arrival services and more. For Phase I we narrowed it to:

> **How may we improve the check-in experience with better kiosk implementation in hotels?**

The refinement was made in three steps, each tied to evidence from our preliminary research (Chapter 2) and fieldwork (Chapter 5).

**1. From the whole arrival experience to check-in.** Of the touchpoints in a hotel stay, check-in (together with check-out) is where we found the highest leverage. It is the moment where manpower pressure, regulatory duty and guest expectations meet. Identity verification is mandatory, it is repetitive, and it is bunched into peak windows that a discretely staffed front desk struggles to absorb.

**2. From "AI and emerging technologies" to kiosks.** Rather than survey every technology, we looked for the one that all stakeholders were already converging on. Guests want to skip queues, hotels are short of front-desk staff, STB and IMDA push and fund digital check-in, and 24 EVA-certified vendors are ready to supply it. Kiosks are a proven technology in other industries and an established one in hotels. A solution built on them has a realistic path to adoption, and the open questions concern *how well* they are deployed.

**3. From "kiosks" to "kiosk implementation".** Our hotel visits showed that the technology alone does not determine success. Kiosk usage varied sharply between properties: about **20%** at one serviced residence we visited, against about **85%** at The Ridge at NUS, which became our benchmark. The difference could not be explained by the kiosk hardware alone. It also came from where the kiosk was placed, whether guests noticed and trusted it, how reliably it worked, whether staff had been trained to support it, and whether the hotel had thought through who its guests were. In other words, **kiosk design is only one small factor in successful kiosk implementation.** Redesigning a kiosk would therefore leave most of the gap unaddressed, so the problem we chose to solve is the implementation of the kiosk in its setting.

### Scope of the refined problem {#problem-scope}

| | Original challenge | Phase I problem statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | The whole arrival experience | The check-in step |
| **Technology** | AI and emerging technologies, open-ended | Self-service kiosks |
| **Core question** | What could be reimagined? | What makes kiosk deployments succeed or fail? |
| **Primary user** | Not specified | Hotel managers and decision-makers |

The original challenge has not been abandoned. Phase II, run together with Stanford ME310, widens the scope again to reimagining the guest experience, building on the implementation principles established in Phase I.
